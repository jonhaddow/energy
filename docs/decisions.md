# Decisions

- **Bright (Hildebrand Glow) for meter data**, not the Loop app: Loop has no public API, while Glowmarkt has a documented one that reads the meter through the DCC. Free.
- **Half-hourly readings stored**, not daily totals: they're the raw data (owning it was a goal), a year is ~35k small rows across both fuels, and day/month totals group by the stored UK `day`.
- **Sheet history kept as monthly totals** (`month_history`), and readings replace a month only once they cover it fully (every day has a real reading: Bright stores missing ones as 0, so counting half hours made gappy months look whole; for gas, which can really be 0 all day, electricity's reading that day counts too, from gas's first real reading on. A gas-only gap later on would then count as 0: rare once Bright has caught up), so a month the meter only partly covers doesn't look like a drop.
- **Backfill 2 windows per hourly run** (18 days per fuel): keeps each run within Workers' free-plan CPU and subrequest limits. About a year of history takes a day.
- **The sheet's "Year 1" and "Year 2" were imported as 2025 and 2026** (Year 2 has data to September, last edited October 2026). Check against the meter data once it backfills; fix by re-importing.
- **Not doing (October 2026)**: notes for life events on the charts (new boiler, insulation), monthly kWh or £ targets, and grid carbon intensity / greener timing. Jon wasn't interested. Also the comparison with Ofgem's typical homes (TDCVs): built (#17), then removed, as Jon wouldn't use it; its table was dropped.
- **Prices typed in on a Settings page** (`tariff`: fuel, start date, unit rate and standing charge in pence with VAT), not fetched: Ecotricity has no API. Prices stay in force until the next one for that fuel; editing is re-entering the same date.
- **Its own look, not budget's** (October 2026): Space Grotesk, warm paper and charcoal with amber for what matters now, flat bordered cards with 6px corners, small-capital labels. Budget's code patterns are still shared; its design isn't. Tried first: budget's design with just an amber accent.
- **No energy provider named in the app** (October 2026): the UI says nothing about Ecotricity, so it isn't tied to one supplier.
- **No month-by-month table on Home** (October 2026): the chart is what gets looked at, and tapping a month shows its numbers in the caption. The chart's text can't be selected, so long presses on a phone scrub it instead.
- **Costs calculated from the prices**, not the sheet's typed-in costs (`month_history.cost_pence`, which assumed 30-day months): every month is priced the same way. A month's kWh is spread evenly over its days, each day at that day's prices, so a mid-month price change splits the month. A month with a day before the first price has no cost.
- **Both = electricity + gas for the months both fuels have**; partial if either is. kWh add up as well as £, so both units work.
- **Laptop layout from 960px** (as budget): pages get `.page.wide` (1040px), and lay out in columns with grid areas. Home: headline on the left, the chart beside them (sticky). Settings: prices beside the form.
- **Why the bill changed**: this year's use priced at last year's prices (each month at the same month a year before) splits the change: use = that minus last year's cost, prices = this year's cost minus that. Standing charges count as prices. Lines that round to £0 are left out.
- **Headline is the last 12 whole months against the 12 before** (rolling), not this calendar year so far: always a full set of seasons, and no empty January. Months without a match a year before (or a price, in £) are left out, so early on it's the months both have. The chart stays calendar years (up to 3).
- **Jul–Dec 2024 imported from the sheet's "Sheet5" tab** (readings; its prices, 22p/55p electricity and 5p/46p gas, are round figures, entered as prices from 1 Jul 2024).
- **Now and Trends pages, not a day/week/month switch** (October 2026): each timescale asks something different. Months answer "less than last year?" (Trends); days and hours answer "what's happening, anything odd?", against a typical day rather than the same day last year, which is mostly weather and weekday noise (Now). Weeks sit on Now as this week against a typical one. Names turned down: "Recent", and "Current" (reads as electric current).
- **New versions load when the app is opened** (October 2026): coming back from the background checks the version and reloads if it changed, as budget does. Mid-use detection still waits for the next tap.
- **Standing charges a year at today's prices**, under the £ headline: what the household pays before using anything. Both adds the two fuels; no figure if either has no price yet.
- **Price changes marked on the chart in £ only**: a diamond on the axis, in that year's colour, where new prices start (each fuel's first prices aren't a change). In kWh prices don't matter, so the chart stays clean. Picking the month names the date.
- **Weather-adjusted gas as gas per heating degree day** (base 15.5°C, the usual UK one), over the same months as the headline: one line under it, with how much colder or warmer it was. Simple ratio, no baseline for hot water split out: over 12 months the summer months barely count either way. A month needs a temperature every day.
- **Location in the database, set in Settings** (a town, found with Open-Meteo's geocoding), not hard-coded. Changing it clears the stored temperatures and the next sync fetches the new place's. Temperatures (`weather_day`) come from Open-Meteo's archive the first time, from the first gas month, then from its forecast API (which has the last few days first), once a day in the hourly sync, whether or not Bright is set up.
- **Quotes priced on the last 12 whole months**, the same 12 for both fuels (to the older of their newest whole months), against today's prices: a year's use at the quote's unit rates plus 365 days of its standing charges. Flat rates, so monthly totals are enough; time-of-use tariffs wait for the half-hourly tariff checker. A quote has both fuels (we have both), and an optional fixed-until date, shown but not used: no exit fees or term maths. Quotes are kept until removed.
- **Energy's e2e and shot server on port 5181**: budget's uses 5180, and parallel sessions ran into each other.
- **Now opens on the newest whole day, not today** (October 2026): Bright's readings arrive a day or so late, so today is nearly always empty. The newest day is shown when its last hour is in; otherwise the day before, with › to the partial one ("so far"). The typical day is the average of the whole days (46+ half hours, not all 0: Bright stores missing readings as 0) in the 4 weeks before, so it follows the season without a year of data. Hours are UK hours, worked out with BST's rule rather than `Intl` (cheap enough for every half hour of a month on Workers' CPU budget).
- **Unusual days: electricity only, against the 30 days' median** (October 2026): over 1.5× or under half the median day. Gas follows the weather (the heating coming on doubles it), so its unusual days wait for weather-adjusted days. Days Bright has nothing for (all 0) get no bar and aren't counted. **This week** is Monday to the newest whole day, against the same days of the 4 weeks before (those with every day in), at each day's prices.
- **Gas's newest whole day also goes by electricity** (October 2026): a day counts as in when its last hour has a reading, but gas is often really 0 late in the evening, so for gas electricity having reached the end of that day (or beyond) counts too. Not the other way round: Bright sometimes gets electricity a day after gas.
- **shadcn-svelte for the polish, kept in the app's look** (October 2026, as in Stash): buttons come from shadcn-svelte (`lib/components/ui`, copied in and restyled with the app's colours and corners). Tailwind is there for those components only, without its reset; the rest stays plain CSS. Pressed states on buttons, segments and links, and pages crossfade (View Transitions).
